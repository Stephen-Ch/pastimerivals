Ted Williams (1941) vs Sandy Koufax (1965)

Ted Williams hit .406 in 1941, and no one has hit .400 since. Sandy Koufax struck out 382 batters in 1965. Both were left-handed. Who wins?

The quick answer

Koufax wins the strikeout battle. Williams still reaches base 4 times in 10.

Williams in 1941

185 hits, 37 home runs, 147 walks and only 27 strikeouts in 606 trips to the plate. He struck out at about half his league's rate and hit home runs at almost 4 times its rate.

Koufax in 1965

382 strikeouts, 71 walks and 26 home runs allowed in 1,297 batters faced. He struck out batters at almost twice his league's rate.

Why so many strikeouts for Williams?

Williams almost never struck out. Koufax struck out almost everyone. The model combines the two, so the result lands in between: higher than Williams' own rate, lower than Koufax's.

Then comes the lefty problem. Batters usually do worse against pitchers who throw from the same side. That pushes Williams' strikeouts up a little more.

Why Williams still gets on base

Williams walked at more than twice his league's rate and hit for power. Koufax kept walks low, but Williams' eye still wins about 1 walk in every 9 trips. Add his hits and he reaches base 40.1% of the time.

How to read these odds

These are chances, not predictions. A 26.5% strikeout chance means about 1 in 4. Over a few at-bats, Williams can easily win.

Questions

Who would win, Williams or Koufax?

Koufax gets more strikeouts. Williams gets on base often enough to call it close.

Is 26.5% strikeouts too high for Williams?

It is far above his real 1941 rate. That is the effect of facing the top strikeout pitcher of his time, left on left. Play it and decide.

Why compare across eras at all?

Because it's fun, and because comparing each player to his own league makes it fair.
