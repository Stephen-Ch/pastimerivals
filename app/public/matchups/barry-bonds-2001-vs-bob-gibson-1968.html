Barry Bonds (2001) vs Bob Gibson (1968)

Two of the most extreme seasons in baseball history. Bonds hit 73 home runs in 2001. Gibson pitched in 1968, a season so tough on hitters that baseball lowered the pitcher's mound the next year. Who wins when they meet?

The quick answer

Bonds reaches base 43.2% of the time, mostly on walks. Gibson cuts his home runs by almost half.

Bonds in 2001

156 hits, 73 home runs, 177 walks and 93 strikeouts in 664 trips to the plate. He hit home runs at 3.5 times his league's rate and walked at 3 times its rate. Pitchers were so afraid of him that they often put him on base on purpose.

Gibson in 1968

268 strikeouts, 62 walks and 11 home runs allowed in 1,161 batters faced. He allowed home runs at about two thirds of his league's rate, in a league where home runs were already rare.

Why the numbers come out this way

We compare each player to his own league, so Gibson's low-scoring season does not make him look better than he was. He was great because he beat even 1968's hitters. Then both players meet on neutral ground, in an average league from 1990 to 2019.

Bonds' walk edge is the biggest number in this matchup. Gibson walked fewer batters than his league, but not by enough to stop Bonds. So Bonds walks about once every 5 trips.

Home runs are where Gibson wins. Bonds homered in 11.0% of his trips in 2001. Against Gibson, that drops to 6.4%.

How to read these odds

These are chances, not predictions. Over a short at-bat, Gibson can strike Bonds out. Over a season, Bonds' walks add up.

Questions

Who would win, Bonds or Gibson?

It depends on what you count. Gibson keeps the ball in the park. Bonds still gets on base more than 4 times in 10.

Why so many walks?

Bonds walked almost 3 times as often as his league. Gibson cuts that edge only a little.

Does the 1968 mound matter?

Indirectly. We compare Gibson to 1968's league, so the high mound helped everyone, not just him.
